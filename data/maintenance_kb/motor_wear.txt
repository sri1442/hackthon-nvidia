FAULT: Drive Motor Wear / Excess Current Draw
GROUP: drive
PARAMETERS: drive_motor_current_a, drive_vib_rms_g, drive_speed_ms

ROOT CAUSES:
- Brush or winding wear increasing electrical resistance and current draw
  for a given load.
- Increased mechanical friction (worn bushings/gearbox) requiring more
  torque, and therefore more current, to maintain commanded speed.
- Partial motor winding short reducing efficiency.

DIAGNOSTIC INDICATORS:
- drive_motor_current_a rising linearly over time while drive_speed_ms
  stays constant is the classic signature of gradual motor wear (increasing
  resistance/friction compensated by the controller).
- drive_vib_rms_g rising in parallel with motor current suggests mechanical
  wear (bearings/gearbox) rather than a purely electrical fault.
- If current rises but vibration stays flat, suspect winding degradation
  over mechanical wear.

RECOMMENDED ACTIONS:
- Motor current 30-45A (warning): schedule motor inspection/service within
  the next maintenance window. Estimated 4-6 hours for motor service or
  replacement.
- Motor current above 45A (critical): immediate action — risk of thermal
  damage to windings. Estimated 4-6 hours for motor replacement.
- Check gearbox lubrication and bushing wear before assuming a full motor
  replacement is required if vibration is only mildly elevated.

SAFETY NOTES:
- Sustained current above hard failure threshold risks winding insulation
  breakdown and potential fire hazard — remove from service immediately.
